Guides to break-even and contribution margin

Each guide answers one question about cost-volume-profit arithmetic, shows the formula, works a numeric example step by step and links the textbook or government source for the definitions.

Suggested reading order

  1. Fixed vs variable costs: how to sort your costs, split mixed costs with the high-low method and spot step costs. Everything else depends on getting this right.
  2. Contribution margin explained: per-unit, total and ratio forms; the contribution-format income statement; how it differs from gross margin.
  3. Break-even in units vs revenue: the two break-even formulas, why to round units up, and weighted-average break-even for a sales mix.
  4. Target profit and margin of safety: volume for a profit goal (before and after tax), the cushion above break-even and degree of operating leverage.

One running example

To make the guides easy to compare, most of them reuse the same illustrative product: a $40 selling price, $25 of variable cost per unit and $5,000 of fixed costs per month. That gives $15 of contribution per unit, a 37.5% contribution margin ratio and a break-even point of about 333.33 units ($13,333.33 of sales). When a guide needs a different situation, such as a service business with no natural unit or two products sold together, it says so and shows the new inputs.
