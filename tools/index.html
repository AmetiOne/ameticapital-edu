Gather these inputs first

  1. Selling price per unit, net of discounts you routinely give and excluding sales tax collected for a government.
  2. Variable cost per unit: costs that rise in proportion to each unit sold, such as materials, packaging, per-unit shipping, sales commissions and card-processing fees.
  3. Fixed costs for one period: costs that stay flat within your normal volume range, such as rent, salaried wages, insurance and software subscriptions. Use the same period (usually a month) everywhere.

Not sure where a cost belongs? The fixed vs variable costs guide walks through classification and splitting mixed costs. For the reasoning behind each formula, start with contribution margin explained.

How the calculators handle edge cases

If variable cost per unit is equal to or greater than price, contribution margin is zero or negative and no volume can reach break-even; the tools say so instead of printing a misleading number. Extreme or non-numeric inputs are rejected. Results show fractional units so you can check the arithmetic; in practice you round break-even units up to the next whole unit.
