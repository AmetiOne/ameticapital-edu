Break-even calculator

Enter one period's fixed costs, a unit selling price and the variable cost per unit. The calculator shows contribution margin, break-even units, break-even revenue and, if you add a target profit, the volume needed to reach it.

Contribution margin per unit = price − variable cost. Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ contribution margin per unit. Break-even revenue = break-even units × price.

Formulas used

Contribution margin per unit = price − variable cost per unit
Contribution margin ratio = contribution margin per unit ÷ price
Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ contribution margin per unit
Break-even revenue = fixed costs ÷ contribution margin ratio
Units for target profit = (fixed costs + target profit) ÷ contribution margin per unit

These are the standard cost-volume-profit relationships described in OpenStax Managerial Accounting §3.2 and in the U.S. Small Business Administration's break-even guidance (sources below).

Worked example (illustrative)

A product sells for $40 and carries $25 of variable cost per unit; fixed costs for the month are $5,000.

  1. Contribution margin per unit = $40 − $25 = $15.
  2. Contribution margin ratio = $15 ÷ $40 = 37.5%.
  3. Break-even units = $5,000 ÷ $15 ≈ 333.33, so 334 whole units. Check: 333 units leave 333 × $15 − $5,000 = −$5; 334 units leave +$10.
  4. Break-even revenue = $5,000 ÷ 0.375 ≈ $13,333.33 (the same as 333.33 × $40).
  5. With a $3,000 target profit: ($5,000 + $3,000) ÷ $15 ≈ 533.33, so 534 units, or about $21,333 of revenue.

How sensitive is break-even to price?

Holding variable cost at $25 and fixed costs at $5,000, small price changes move break-even a lot because they change contribution per unit one-for-one:

$36$11454.55 → 455$16,363.64
$38$13384.62 → 385$14,615.38
$40$15333.33 → 334$13,333.33
$42$17294.12 → 295$12,352.94
$44$19263.16 → 264$11,578.95

A 10% price cut (from $40 to $36) raises break-even volume by about 36% in this example. Fixed costs behave linearly instead: at $15 contribution, $4,000 of fixed costs needs 266.67 units and $6,000 needs 400. Changing one input at a time like this is the sensitivity analysis described in OpenStax §3.3.

Before you enter your own numbers

  • Use one period for fixed costs and think about volume for that same period.
  • Count only costs that truly vary per unit as variable; see fixed vs variable costs.
  • Use one currency and keep sales tax out of price.
  • Remember the model assumes a constant price, constant variable cost and fixed costs that stay flat across the volume range.

What the result does not tell you

Break-even is an operating-profit concept, not a cash forecast. Loan principal repayments, inventory build-up, owner draws, taxes and payment timing are outside the model. It also does not tell you whether the market will buy 334 units at $40. Treat the output as a structured way to test assumptions, not as a plan.

Is break-even the same as profit?

No. At break-even, total contribution equals fixed costs, so modeled operating profit is zero. Every unit above break-even adds one unit's contribution margin to operating profit.

Should I use break-even units or break-even revenue?

Use units when you sell one product with a clear unit. Use revenue when you sell many products or services; the units vs revenue guide explains both.
